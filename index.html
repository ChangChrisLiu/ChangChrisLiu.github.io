Chang (Chris) Liu

Ph.D. student at Texas A&M University

Robot learning for reliable, contact-rich manipulation.

I’m a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at Texas A&M University, advised by Dr. Minghui Zheng in the Control and Robotics Lab.

I study how robots can learn from experience and recover from mistakes. My work connects vision-language-action (VLA) models, human corrections, and structured manipulation skills, using the disassembly of end-of-life electronics as a real-world testbed.

Before my Ph.D., I worked in industrial 3D printing at HeyGears. Supporting automation in the field shaped the question behind my research: how can we make capable robot policies work reliably outside a carefully prepared demo?

PACER CoRL 2026

Learning from robot experience and human corrections

PACER improves VLA policies through native supervised fine-tuning, using robot rollouts, human corrections, and clean demonstrations. Task progress and execution outcomes determine how each valid action chunk contributes to learning.

87/100 successful approach-and-handoff trials, compared with 54/100 for vanilla post-SFT, on a held-out UR5e benchmark across five component classes.

SELF-VLA Preprint · 2026

Connecting learned policies with disassembly skills

An agentic VLA framework that combines learned approach and recovery policies with explicit manipulation skills. The robot coordinates perception, action, and skill execution to handle contact-rich disassembly.

RAISE Resources, Conservation & Recycling · 2026

A complete system for end-of-life phone disassembly

A collaborative system that integrates adaptive cutting, vision-based robotic sorting, and battery removal. My work focused on the UR5e sorting station, including perception, hand-eye calibration, and system integration with Idaho National Laboratory.
